# Visual Constitution

Aim for first-glance attraction through contrast, hierarchy, and image relevance, not decoration.

## Non-negotiable

- Use 1080x1440 (3:4). Preserve a consistent safe area and page number position.
- Make the cover readable at feed size: one dominant promise, one supporting label, one clear focal image or graphic.
- Use two neutrals plus one accent; add a warning color only when semantically necessary.
- Alternate light and dark pages or full-bleed and structured pages to create rhythm.
- Keep hierarchy obvious at a glance: headline, evidence or object, action detail.
- Use real product, place, food, outfit, interface, or state images when inspection matters.
- Keep cards square or lightly rounded. Avoid nested cards, pill-heavy layouts, bokeh blobs, generic gradient backgrounds, and decorative 3D objects unrelated to the topic.

## Direction families

### Editorial

Use for stories, travel, books, emotion, fashion, beauty, and image-led reviews. Favor a strong crop, generous negative space, serif/sans contrast, asymmetric balance, and short captions.

### Swiss

Use for tutorials, checklists, tools, office, study, finance, and comparisons. Favor a modular grid, large numerals, labels, data hierarchy, hard rules, and restrained icons.

Mix them only with a clear reason: for example, an editorial cover followed by Swiss instruction pages.

## Image strategy

- User material comes first. Preserve source notes and do not retouch evidence beyond honest presentation.
- Use generated images for concepts or clearly illustrative scenes; label them when a reader could mistake them for documentary evidence.
- Never use an AI image to impersonate a real visit, wear test, product test, recipe result, medical outcome, or parenting experience.
- When assets are missing, replace a photo-dependent direction with typography, diagrams, maps, ingredient grids, interface crops supplied by the user, or an explicit shot list. Do not silently invent proof.

## Quality check

At feed-size preview, verify: the title fits; the focal point is immediate; accent color is sparse; body text is not dense; adjacent pages differ in composition; no page looks dirty, empty, or like a generic template.
